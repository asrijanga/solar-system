# SS-10 · Real 3D terrain that sharpens as you zoom

Status: **in progress: terrain in the app, first cut** · Release 3 · 2026-09-25

As a learner, I want the Moon's surface to be real 3D terrain that gets sharper as I come closer, so that craters and mountains are shapes I can look across, not a picture on a ball.

## Why now

On 2026-09-25 the owner, looking at SS-8's relief on an iPhone, said the surface "looks like a flat picture". It was right for two reasons.

- **The geometry is coarse.** SS-8 lights true slopes, but on a smooth sphere displaced at 10.7 km spacing, so up close there is no shape to look across.
- **The terrain never gets closer.** The camera stops 870 km up.

The owner chose real terrain geometry as the next step ("Real terrain mesh up close"), with cast shadows after it.

## Owner direction, 2026-09-25: geometry, not images

"I don't want images … it's a simulation … a faithful recreation of reality … so draw polygons." So:

- **Shape is polygons only.** Every vertex sits on a measured elevation. How sharp the surface is up close is set by the finest measurement available for that spot, never by an image.
- **No detail is added beyond what was measured.** Interpolating between measured points is allowed; inventing bumps between them is not (`CLAUDE.md`, "Never invent data").
- **Brightness is a measured property of each point** (normal albedo, from LOLA's laser or Clementine's calibrated reflectance), carried per vertex or per tile. It is never a photograph with light baked in.
- **Lighting comes only from the Sun and the geometry.**

## Spike result (the plan's first gate)

**Question:** can NASA-AMMOS's 3DTilesRendererJS stream our Moon in `WebGPURenderer`, under our TSL photometry?

**First answer: yes.**

- **Library:** `3d-tiles-renderer` 0.5.3, whose `QuantizedMeshPlugin` loads Cesium quantized-mesh terrain. Its ellipsoid is set to the Moon's 1737.4 km sphere.
- **Tiles:** `pipeline/quantized_mesh.py` encodes LOLA `LDEM_64` into quantized-mesh tiles: levels 0–3 globally, down to level 7 (0.66 km spacing) around Albategnius. That is 316 tiles, 24 MB.
- **Scene placement:** tile positions are body-fixed metres, placed in the scene by the epoch's body-to-scene rotation and a 1/1000 scale.
- **Rendering:** it renders through the `RenderPipeline` scene pass with a Lommel–Seeliger material from `core/photometry.ts`, on SwiftShader, and all tiles load. See `ss10-spike-60km.png` and `ss10-spike-15km.png`: Albategnius's rim and central peak are solid shapes, and the curved horizon shows at 15 km.

**Second pass: the finest LOLA data.** The same crater from LOLA `LDEM_512` (59 m), fetched as a 1.1 GB byte range of the PDS tile covering 45°S to 0°, 0–90°E.

- **Registration:** it matches the 474 m grid with r = 0.9998 and a mean difference of 0.3 m.
- **Tile depth:** level 10 (83 m vertex spacing) over the crater, and level 11 (41 m, interpolating the 59 m data) at the central peak. That is 1,741 tiles, 130 MB.
- **Result:** at 15 km the floor shows craters a few hundred metres across; at 4 km the central peak is a ridge in the foreground (`ss10-spike-59m-15km.png`, `ss10-spike-59m-4km.png`).

## First cut in the app (2026-09-25)

The owner said "Recreate the terrain", so the terrain now replaces the sphere in every relief view, including the app.

- **Tiles** (`npm run pipeline:tiles`, `pipeline/terrain_tiles.py`):
  - **Everywhere:** levels 0–5 from LOLA `LDEM_64`, vertices 2.7 km apart at level 5.
  - **Around Albategnius:** levels 6–11 from the `LDEM_512` byte range, 83 m apart over the crater and 41 m at its peak.
  - **Size:** 4,291 tiles, 353 MB, built in about a minute.
  - **Vertex normals:** octahedral, 8-bit, from central differences of the same measured grid over each tile's vertex spacing. Neighbouring tiles share them at their edges, so there are no seams from per-tile normals.
- **Hosting until object storage exists:** CI and the Pages deploy build the tiles from the pinned, checksummed sources on every run (2.2 GB of sources, cached by `actions/cache`) and publish them with the site. They are never committed.
- **Material:** the same TSL Lommel–Seeliger as the sphere, now in `createMoonMaterial`.
  - Each fragment's body-fixed position comes from its world position.
  - The normal is the vertex normal.
  - The horizon test uses the fragment's true height.
  - Albedo and gaps are sampled by position, as before.
  - The SS-8 normal and height maps are no longer loaded.
- **Level of detail:** refines to 1 drawing-buffer pixel of geometric error (a quarter of a vertex spacing), against the library's recommended 2. So vertices are at most about 4 pixels apart wherever the data goes that deep.
- **Camera:** OrbitControls now stops above the ground under the camera, at an altitude set by the finest data measured there (`core/terrain.ts`, `minAltitudeKm`): the view spans at least 40 vertex spacings.
  - About 114 km over the 2.7 km global grid.
  - About 2 km over Albategnius.
  - Zoom and drag speeds scale with altitude, updated at the start and end of each gesture, never per frame.
- **Captures:** every check passes on the terrain, and every negative control still fails.
  - The walls check at Albategnius: west 73.3 against east 55.8.
  - The night check still holds at 6.5°.
  - At full phase the relief is within 2 levels on 99.1% of the disc.
  - The seam ratio is 1.00.
  - The captures are deterministic across two renders.
  - New for eyes: `moon-albategnius-60km`.
- **Allocation:** 0 B from `src/` over 600 frames. The tile library's traversal allocates about 53 KB per frame on its own. It is recorded in the same bucket as three.js, which the gate reports but does not fail.
- **Bundle:** 247.1 KB of 400 KB.

## What gets built

1. **Data inventory first** (README, "Gather first, then build"): the elevation half of `docs/data/moon.md` completed and sized.
   - LOLA `LDEM_128` (237 m, 4.2 GB), `LDEM_256` (118 m, 17 GB) and `LDEM_512` (59 m, 68 GB).
   - SLDEM2015, LOLA with Kaguya, 59 m, ±60°.
   - The LOLA polar DEMs.
   - Where they overlap, choose by stated criteria and blend over a stated band, as SS-6b did.
2. **Terrain tile pyramid** from the best elevation per region: quantized-mesh with octahedral normals from the full-resolution data, and a stated geometric error per level.
3. **In the app:** the streamed terrain replaces the sphere.
   - The albedo and normal maps are sampled by body-fixed position, as now, so lighting stays unbaked and every existing photometry check still applies.
   - The camera can come down to a few kilometres. The real camera is SS-11; until then, orbit controls with a minimum altitude.
4. **Checks:**
   - The existing photometry, Gazetteer, relief and night checks, on the streamed terrain.
   - A new seam check at tile boundaries.
   - Crater depth against a published value.
   - A capture at every level-of-detail switch distance.
5. **Cast shadows** (SS-8 part 2, horizon maps) follow immediately after.

## Hosting (owner action needed)

The pyramid is gigabytes: about 6–8 GB for the whole Moon at 118 m, and about 30 GB at 59 m. That is far past git and GitHub Pages (about 1 GB). The plan already names object storage (README, "You do not need a server yet").

- **Proposed:** a Cloudflare R2 bucket with public read, CORS allowing `https://asrijanga.github.io`, and an API token with object read and write on that bucket.
- **Environment variables:** `R2_ACCOUNT_ID`, `R2_ACCESS_KEY_ID`, `R2_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY`, `R2_BUCKET`, `R2_PUBLIC_URL`, added in the environment settings.
- **Until it exists,** everything is built and checked against locally served tiles.

## Ground truth

- **Heights:** Chang'E-1 extremes (as SS-8), plus crater depths with published sources, cited when chosen.
- **Positions:** IAU Gazetteer features must land on the right landforms in 3D views.
- **Photometry:** per-pixel checks as now.
