# SS-10 · Real 3D terrain that sharpens as you zoom

Status: **spec, awaiting approval** · Release 3 · 2026-09-25

As a learner, I want the Moon's surface to be real 3D terrain that gets sharper as I come closer, so that craters and mountains are shapes I can look across, not a picture on a ball.

## Why now

On 2026-09-25 the owner, looking at SS-8's relief on an iPhone, said the surface "looks like a flat picture". It was right for two reasons.

- **The geometry is coarse.** SS-8 lights true slopes, but on a smooth sphere displaced at 10.7 km spacing, so up close there is no shape to look across.
- **The terrain never gets closer.** The camera stops 870 km up.

The owner chose real terrain geometry as the next step ("Real terrain mesh up close"), with cast shadows after it.

## Spike result (the plan's first gate)

**Question:** can NASA-AMMOS's 3DTilesRendererJS stream our Moon in `WebGPURenderer`, under our TSL photometry?

**First answer: yes.**

- **Library:** `3d-tiles-renderer` 0.5.3, whose `QuantizedMeshPlugin` loads Cesium quantized-mesh terrain. Its ellipsoid is set to the Moon's 1737.4 km sphere.
- **Tiles:** `pipeline/quantized_mesh.py` encodes LOLA `LDEM_64` into quantized-mesh tiles: levels 0–3 globally, down to level 7 (0.66 km spacing) around Albategnius. That is 316 tiles, 24 MB.
- **Scene placement:** tile positions are body-fixed metres, placed in the scene by the epoch's body-to-scene rotation and a 1/1000 scale.
- **Rendering:** it renders through the `RenderPipeline` scene pass with a Lommel–Seeliger material from `core/photometry.ts`, on SwiftShader, and all tiles load. See `ss10-spike-60km.png` and `ss10-spike-15km.png`: Albategnius's rim and central peak are solid shapes, and the curved horizon shows at 15 km.

**Not yet shown by the spike** (these gate adoption):

| Question | How it will be answered |

## What gets built

1. **Data inventory first** (README, "Gather first, then build"): the elevation half of `docs/data/moon.md` completed and sized.
   - LOLA `LDEM_128` (237 m, 4.2 GB), `LDEM_256` (118 m, 17 GB) and `LDEM_512` (59 m, 68 GB).
   - SLDEM2015, LOLA with Kaguya, 59 m, ±60°.
   - The LOLA polar DEMs.
   - Where they overlap, choose by stated criteria and blend over a stated band, as SS-6b did.
2. **Terrain tile pyramid** from the best elevation per region: quantized-mesh with octahedral normals from the full-resolution data, and a stated geometric error per level.
3. **In the app:** the streamed terrain replaces the sphere.
   - The albedo and normal maps are sampled by body-fixed position, as now, so lighting stays unbaked and every existing photometry check still applies.
   - The camera can come down to a few kilometres. The real camera is SS-11; until then, orbit controls with a minimum altitude.
4. **Checks:**
   - The existing photometry, Gazetteer, relief and night checks, on the streamed terrain.
   - A new seam check at tile boundaries.
   - Crater depth against a published value.
   - A capture at every level-of-detail switch distance.
5. **Cast shadows** (SS-8 part 2, horizon maps) follow immediately after.

## Hosting (owner action needed)

The pyramid is gigabytes: about 6–8 GB for the whole Moon at 118 m, and about 30 GB at 59 m. That is far past git and GitHub Pages (about 1 GB). The plan already names object storage (README, "You do not need a server yet").

- **Proposed:** a Cloudflare R2 bucket with public read, CORS allowing `https://asrijanga.github.io`, and an API token with object read and write on that bucket.
- **Environment variables:** `R2_ACCOUNT_ID`, `R2_ACCESS_KEY_ID`, `R2_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY`, `R2_BUCKET`, `R2_PUBLIC_URL`, added in the environment settings.
- **Until it exists,** everything is built and checked against locally served tiles.

## Ground truth

- **Heights:** Chang'E-1 extremes (as SS-8), plus crater depths with published sources, cited when chosen.
- **Positions:** IAU Gazetteer features must land on the right landforms in 3D views.
- **Photometry:** per-pixel checks as now.

## Not verified by the spike

Everything in the table above, plus albedo on the streamed tiles, which the spike rendered in uniform grey on purpose.

The plan's heaviest story: 3 to 5 sessions, plus pipeline time and uploads.
