## W2 · Constants and ephemeris

`pipeline/ephemeris_mars.py` writes `public/data/mars/ephemeris.json`. Run it with `npm run pipeline:ephemeris:mars`; CI re-derives the output and diffs it.

**Kernels:** the Moon's pinned `naif0012.tls`, `de440s.bsp`, `pck00011.tpc` and `gm_de440.tpc`, plus `mar099s.bsp`.
- **What `mar099s.bsp` holds:** Mars, Phobos and Deimos relative to the Mars barycentre, from 1995 to 2049 (NAIF, June 2025, per its `.cmt`).
- **Pin:** size checked against the server's 67,594,240 bytes; SHA-256 pinned at first retrieval.

**Written:**
- **Mars:**
  - Radii 3396.19 × 3396.19 × 3376.20 km (`BODY499_RADII`).
  - GM from `gm_de440.tpc`.
  - `IAU_MARS` body frame: planetocentric, east-positive.
- **Phobos and Deimos:** their radii and `IAU_PHOBOS` / `IAU_DEIMOS` frames.
- **At each canonical epoch:**
  - The Sun's and Earth's directions and distances.
  - The rotation into `IAU_MARS`.
  - Phobos's and Deimos's positions from Mars's centre, and their rotations.
  - Mars's season as its solar longitude Ls.

**Epochs.** The same two instants as the Moon's, so the whole app runs on one clock:

| Epoch | UTC | Ls | Sun distance | Mars's season |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `full-2026-01` | 2026-01-03 10:00 | 200.2° | 1.426 AU | Southern spring |
| `first-quarter-2026-01` | 2026-01-26 05:00 | 214.0° | 1.406 AU | Southern spring |

Ls 180–360° is Mars's dust-storm season. Whether the surface was clear on those dates is for W3 to establish from MARCI, not to assume.

### Checked against JPL Horizons

Fixture: `test/fixtures/horizons-mars.json`, with the requests recorded. Test: `pipeline/test_ephemeris_mars.py`.

| Check | Measured agreement | Tolerance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sub-solar point seen from Earth | 5e-5° in longitude, below 1e-6° in latitude | 0.1° (the Moon's) |
| The shipped vectors (Sun direction × rotation matrix), against the sub-solar point seen from Phobos | 8e-5° | 0.1° |
| Phobos's and Deimos's positions from Mars's centre | below 1 m. Horizons uses the same `mar099` solution, so this proves the reading, not the orbit | 1 km |

**Two conventions bit here, both recorded for the next world:**

- **Horizons gives Mars's longitudes west-positive and its latitudes planetodetic**, from its own header: "Target pole/equ : IAU_MARS {West-longitude positive}" and "Latitude is the angle between the equatorial plane and the line perpendicular to the reference ellipsoid".
  - The test converts both. For the Moon, Horizons used east longitudes.
  - A control test shows the check fails without the conversion, so it can see the difference.
- **Light time.** Earth sees Mars as it was about 20 minutes earlier, and in that time Mars turns about 5°.
  - The first version of the test compared the app's vectors with the sub-solar point seen from Earth. It failed by 4.87°.
  - For the Moon (1.3 s) the difference had been invisible.
  - The app draws Mars as it is at the instant. So those vectors are checked against Horizons seen from Phobos, where the light time is 0.03 s.
  - The sub-solar point seen from Earth is still checked against Horizons from Earth.

## W3 · True albedo, part 1: the map

**Owner decisions, 2026-09-29:**
- Time: "Long-term average (Recommended)".
- Map, after the side-by-side (`ss14-albedo-candidates.png`): "OMEGA, checked by TES (Recommended)".

**The comparison** (`pipeline/compare_mars_products.py`), over central Valles Marineris beside the HRSC–MOLA relief lit from one side:
- **Viking MDIM 2.1:** the Viking Sun is baked into every canyon wall. Rejected for albedo (CLAUDE.md: never bake lighting).
- **TES bolometric albedo:** measured, but 7.4 km, with a gridding cross-hatch and a seam near 9.5° S.
- **OMEGA 1.08 µm Lambert albedo** (Ody et al. 2012), averaged over 2004–2010:
  - Clean on flat ground at 1.5 km.
  - Steep walls keep some shading, because its cos(i) is taken to the flat surface, not the slope.
  - Faint stripes where orbit strips meet.

**Built** (`pipeline/mars.py`, `npm run pipeline:mars`; CI re-derives it and diffs it):
- `public/data/mars/albedo.webp`, 8192 × 4096 (2.6 km per pixel at the equator):
  - OMEGA's 1.08 µm Lambert albedo, area-averaged from 40 px/deg, stored as byte = 255 √(A / 1.0).
  - 1.0 is the label's maximum. 6 pixels exceed it and are clipped, all at 85.3° S, where the TES fill (up to 1.18 there, over the south polar ice) makes up 54–69% of each pixel.
- **Encoding:** the Moon's rule (at most 6 MB, PSNR ≥ 42 dB) chose WebP q70, 0.43 MB, 43.4 dB.
  - On the Moon, q70 under the earlier 40 dB rule looked blocky in smooth maria. The 42 dB rule chose it here too.
  - Whether it looks blocky on Mars is for the owner's eyes once Mars is drawn (W7).
- **Gaps: 2.9% of OMEGA's map**, most in 0–30° N (5.6%) as streaks between orbits.
  - Filled from TES × (OMEGA / TES), with the ratio averaged over the surrounding 5.1° where OMEGA measured completely.
  - Filled pixels keep TES's 7.4 km cells. `albedo-source.png` records the share of each pixel that came from TES (`ss14-albedo-map.png`, orange).
- **How well TES predicts OMEGA**, from held-out cells OMEGA measured (1%, fixed seed):

| Zone | Cells | r | Mean error | RMS error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Within 60° | 26,485 | 0.978 | +0.6% | 7.3% |
| Poleward of 60° | 13,451 | 0.668 | +7.1% | 35.8% |

  - Near the poles the two disagree: TES observed 1999–2002 and OMEGA 2004–2010, and seasonal frost differs between them. Poleward of 60° the fill is a different measurement, not a prediction of OMEGA.
  - 2.3% of the map beyond 60° S and 1.5% beyond 60° N is filled this way.
- **The two instruments overall** (TES cells OMEGA measured completely): r 0.949 within 60°, and OMEGA = 1.41 × TES. Mars is brighter at 1.08 µm than in TES's broad band.

**Checks** (`pipeline/test_mars.py`, new):
- Named albedo features where the IAU Gazetteer puts them (`test/fixtures/iau-gazetteer-mars.json`: 0–360°, "Positive East"):
  - Syrtis Major and Mare Acidalium (dark, about 0.14) against Tharsis, Hellas and Arabia Terra (bright, 0.33–0.43).
  - Every dark feature is at most 0.6 × every bright one.
- The same check on the map mirrored in longitude fails, so it can see a mirrored map.
- The fill check is recorded and passes: r > 0.95 and |mean error| < 2% within 60°.
- The texture's mean albedo is 0.294 ± 0.02, the label's mean.

**Not yet** (at part 1): visible light and colour, which part 2 adds below. Part 1's map is now `pipeline/mars_omega.py`, which `pipeline/mars.py` uses as its anchor.

## W3 · True albedo, part 2: visible light and colour

**HRSC's global colour mosaic** (Michael et al. 2025; `pipeline/hrsc_sources.json`, 24 files pinned by SHA-256):
- Blue 440, green 530, red 750 and infrared 970 nm, each six orthographic faces.
- Assembled onto one 8192 × 4096 grid. Each pixel comes from the face whose centre is nearest. Where faces overlap they agree to 0.34% (green) to 0.64% (blue), median.
- **Its large-scale brightness is detrended**, as its paper says. Syrtis Major / Tharsis is 0.69 at 750 nm against OMEGA's 0.34. HRSC's 970 nm band correlates with OMEGA at only 0.50 within 60°.

**The guided blend** (`pipeline/mars.py`; He, Sun and Tang 2013):
- In every window of 47 px (2.07°), the near-infrared albedo is fitted as a line in HRSC's 970 nm band to OMEGA's 1.08 µm albedo, with TES in OMEGA's gaps.
- The result keeps OMEGA's light and dark beyond the window and HRSC's edges within it. Regulariser 1e-4, floor 0.02 (no pixel reached it).
- The other bands are HRSC's own ratios to 970 nm, so the contrast falls toward blue as HRSC measured it.

| Against OMEGA (+TES) | r, within 60° | RMS difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HRSC 970 nm alone, 8 px/deg | 0.501 | 27.5% |
| Blend, 8 px/deg | 0.989 | 5.1% |
| Blend, 1 px/deg | 0.997 | 2.6% |

- The fitted slope's median is 0.24: within a window, HRSC's variation is scaled down to what OMEGA measured.
- 1% of windows have a negative slope, where the two disagree locally (OMEGA's orbit stripes, HRSC's shading as acquired). There the fine detail is inverted.
- OMEGA's vertical orbit stripes remain at large scale, as in option C.

**Built** (`npm run pipeline:mars`, about 7 min; CI re-derives and diffs it, with the 24 HRSC files added to its cache):
- `albedo.webp`, 8192 × 4096, lossless WebP, 6.25 MB:
  - Reflectance at 530 nm, byte = 255 √(R / 1.2). Median 0.183, mean 0.193, none clipped.
  - The Moon's rule (at most 6 MiB, PSNR ≥ 42 dB) picked q70 at 47.2 dB overall and 44 dB over Valles Marineris. That still smeared the canyon into 16-pixel blocks: its detail is about 6 bytes RMS. Even q95 stayed blocky (`ss14-encoding-valles.png`). Owner: lossless.
- `albedo-colour.png`, 2048 × 1024, lossless: R(750)/R(530), R(440)/R(530), R(970)/R(530). Medians 1.84, 0.56, 1.53.
- `albedo-source.png`: the share of each pixel's anchor filled from TES, as in part 1 (1.1 million pixels).
- `ss14-albedo-blend.png`: the result in display colour (the Moon's method, 5772 K sunlight, CIE 1931, sRGB).
- **Checks** (`pipeline/test_mars.py`):
  - The Gazetteer features at 530 nm: dark at most 0.107 (Syrtis Major), bright at least 0.196 (Hellas). The ratio is 0.55, within the unchanged 0.6.
  - The mirrored control still fails.
  - The fill check is unchanged.
  - **Replaced:** "mean albedo 0.294 ± 0.02" (OMEGA's 1.08 µm label mean) does not apply at 530 nm. In its place, the blend must keep OMEGA's large scale: r > 0.99 and |mean difference| < 1% at 1 px/deg within 60° (measured 0.997 and +0.17%). It is a new threshold, for the owner's approval.
  - The source map matches its manifest.

**Not yet:** the absolute scale is OMEGA's 1.08 µm Lambert albedo carried to 530 nm by HRSC's ratios. Checking it against Mars's published brightness needs Mars drawn with its photometry (W7).

## W4 · Terrain for the website

**Owner decision, 2026-10-02:** **"Own data repo, 1.3 km (Recommended)"**. Mars's tiles go to a data repository of Mars's own, with its own Pages site and its own 1 GB (`docs/world-recipe.md`, W4). Levels 0–7 cover the whole of Mars, with vertices 1.31 km apart at level 7. The owner creates the repository.

### What was found (2026-10-02)

- **The USGS HRSC–MOLA blend's heights are above MOLA's areoid**, not the sphere. Against MOLA `MEGT`, in bins MOLA measured, the median difference is 0.0 m (RMS 48 m). Against the sphere it is off by kilometres.
- **But the blend cannot be a source.**
  - It does not record which pixels are HRSC stereo and which are MOLA interpolated between tracks. That is only in a PDF.
  - Its MOLA part is the interpolated 463 m grid.
  - Its own page says the blending "did not always work well".
  - It breaks "provenance kept per region" (README, "Combine every available source").
- **MOLA alone is mostly interpolation at 1.3 km near the equator.** Its label: "about 55% of bins contain at least one MOLA shot" at 3.7 km, with "gaps … of up to 12 km between profiles".
- **The HRSC team's stereo DTMs are at PDS Geosciences** (`docs/data/mars.md`):
  - 1,370 orbit strips at 50–200 m, about 35 GB.
  - Heights are above the same GMM3 areoid as MOLA's, each strip with its footprint.
  - None are in ESA's archive.

### The composite, built like the Moon's SLDEM (SS-10d)

**Sources, best first, in one frame (MOLA's IAU2000 frame and GMM3 areoid):**
1. **HRSC stereo** (`DA4`), wherever a strip covers the sample:
   - Each strip is registered to MOLA by one height offset, fitted against the MOLA bins with laser shots inside its footprint (`MEGC` > 0). The offset and RMS of each fit are recorded.
   - Where strips overlap, their median is used, and the disagreement is recorded.
2. **MOLA, measured:** MEGDR 128 px/deg bins with at least one shot.
3. **MOLA, interpolated between tracks:** the MEGDR's own interpolation, where nothing else measured.
   - Interpolating between measured points is allowed (SS-10); it is marked as such in the provenance map, never presented as measured.

**Rules for the build:**
- **Edges:** HRSC fades into MOLA across a 5 km band inside each strip's footprint (the band the USGS blend used).
- **Radius:** radius = MOLA areoid (`MEGA`) + height.
- **Provenance:** a map of HRSC, MOLA measured and MOLA interpolated, published beside the tiles.
- **Strips not used:** a strip whose fit RMS against MOLA is more than 3× the median RMS over all strips is left out, with its name and fit recorded (owner, 2026-10-02). The USGS blend dropped two strips for the same reason, h0927 and h0938.

**Tiles and hosting:**
- Quantized-mesh tiles, levels 0–7, gzip. The Moon's tiling (`tools/terrain`), made general for any body.
- The data repository's own Pages workflow checks out this repository at a pinned commit, builds the tiles and deploys them. No secret crosses repositories.
- The 35 GB of strips are streamed, one at a time, into the composite and discarded. The composite is cached.

**Checks:**
- Olympus Mons summit and Hellas floor against MOLA's label extremes (21,171 m and −8,177 m).
- The Gazetteer's named features, where the relief puts them, with a mirrored control.
- MOLA-measured bins reproduced within the fit RMS. The USGS blend as an independent comparison, with the median and RMS recorded.
- A build over budget fails (1 GB).

- **Overlaps:** 23.8 million bins have two or more strips. Their spread is 18.6 m median and 87 m at the 95th percentile.
- **Provenance:** `public/data/mars/terrain-source.webp`, 8 px/deg, lossless. Every bin's exact source is kept beside the composite.

## W5 · Local mode: everything measured, on demand (spec, for the owner's approval)

**Owner, 2026-10-03:** "I can't run locally yet but let's build it through and not skip".

`npm run local` serves Mars as it serves the Moon (SS-10c):
- **On demand:** each terrain tile is built the first time a view needs it, from the publishers' own files.
- **Fetching:** only the blocks a tile covers are fetched, by HTTP range request where the server allows it.
- **Cache:** everything fetched or built is kept in `.cache/local/mars/`.

The Mars page that will show these tiles is W7. Until then the server answers for them and is tested by machine.

### Sources, coarse to fine (`docs/data/mars.md`)

All heights are above the same areoid: MOLA's GMM3, the "Mars 2000 Datum".

| Levels | Vertex spacing | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4 | 167–10 km | MOLA MEGDR 16 px/deg (3.7 km), 33 MB, downloaded whole |
| 5–6 | 5.2–2.6 km | MOLA MEGDR 64 px/deg (0.93 km), by block |
| 7–12 | 1.3 km–41 m | HRSC stereo strips (50–200 m) where they cover, else MOLA MEGDR 128 px/deg (463 m), by block |
| 13–17 | 20–1.3 m | HiRISE DTMs (1–2 m) at their 1,315 sites, else the level-12 sources |

**How the sources are read:**
- **Poles:** beyond 88°, where the cylindrical MEGDR stops, MOLA's polar stereographic MEGDR at 512 px/deg (115 m) supplies MOLA's part.
- **HRSC above its native resolution:** levels 7 and 8 read each strip box-averaged to at most half the vertex spacing, not one pixel per vertex.
- **Radius:** the areoid radius (MEGDR `mega`, 16 px/deg) plus the height. Tiles are on the 3,396 km sphere, as the website's.

### Registration, before a finer product is used

- **HRSC:** each strip uses its W4 fit, the offset to MOLA from `public/data/mars/terrain.json`. The 26 strips left out there are left out here.
- **HiRISE:** each model is checked against MOLA's laser shots, the 128 px/deg bins with shots, inside its footprint. It is used only when that holds. The rule is the Moon's for Kaguya (SS-10c): the mean difference is within 5 m, over at least half the bins. Verdicts and numbers are kept on disk.

### Edges

- **HRSC:** fades into MOLA across 5 km inside its coverage, as on the website (W4). Local mode and the website then agree at 1.3 km.
- **HiRISE:** falls through per vertex where it has no measurement, as Kaguya does on the Moon. Nothing is filled in.

### Checks (machine)

Unit tests, as for the Moon's local mode:
- **File names and URLs** match the PDS and HiRISE listings.
- **Sample positions** match the labels for each projection: equirectangular, sinusoidal and polar stereographic.
- **The level ladder and its availability:** HiRISE levels only where a model exists.
- **Fall-through:** an unmeasured height is refused rather than invented.
- **Agreement with the website:** at level 7, a tile built by local mode equals the website's tile over the same ground, within the 1 m height quantisation, where both use the same sources.

CI cannot run local mode itself, because it downloads gigabytes. It runs the tests.

### Not in W5

- **CTX DTMs:** there is no published, archived CTX terrain product. They are per-site community builds, so not a source here.
- **The labelled approximation below HiRISE** is W6, which is optional.
