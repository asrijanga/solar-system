# SS-16 · Mercury

Status: **in progress** · W1 (inventory) merged 2026-10-05 (PR #57); W2 (constants and ephemeris) merged 2026-10-07 (PR #58); W3 (albedo) built 2026-10-08: MD3, with MDR cross-calibrated where MD3 did not measure

As a learner, I want to visit Mercury, built from every mission that measured it, the way the Moon and Mars were.

Mercury is built by the eight stories of `docs/world-recipe.md`, one PR each. It has no air, so W6 (atmosphere) does not apply.

## Owner decisions

- 2026-10-08, W3, the albedo file's size (`ss16-albedo-encoding.png`): **"Lossless 8192, 12.4 MB (Recommended)"**. Lossy WebP smeared the plains into 16-pixel blocks even at q95, as on Mars. Mercury's cap is 13 MB.
- 2026-10-08, W3, after the residual-shading comparison (`ss16-albedo-candidates.png`, `ss16-albedo-shading.png`): **"MD3 colour (Recommended)"**. Then, when MD3 turned out to stop at 43.8° S: **"MDR there, cross-calibrated (Recommended)"**. MD3 north of about 40° S. South of it, MDR's same three filters, scaled band by band to MD3 where both overlap (fit recorded), blended across 38–43° S, with provenance kept per region. Wherever neither measured stays a gap.
- 2026-10-05, after SS-14 W8: "should we do separate pages for the moons". Answered with a recommendation: Mercury next, because Phobos's and Deimos's best data are not yet open. Then **"Next story"**.

## W1 · Inventory

`docs/data/mercury.md`: every dataset for Mercury's elevation, albedo, imagery, photometry, geometry and names.
- **Reachability:** each dataset checked from the cloud on 2026-10-05; all are reachable.
- **Conventions:** read from the files' own headers, PDS3 labels and the MDIS archive's own documentation (`MDIS_CDR_RDRSIS.PDF`).

**What it found:**
- **Three reference spheres:** 2439.4 km (USGS DEM and every MDIS map), 2440.0 km (MLA) and pck00011's 2440.53 × 2438.26 km ellipsoid. They differ by up to 1.13 km and must be reconciled before any blend.
- **Every MDIS map is normalised to i = 30°, e = 0°, g = 30°,** with the angles taken on the equipotential surface. Shading from slopes therefore stays in maps made with the Sun low:
  - The BDR (incidence near 74°) and HIE/HIW (78–86°) are rejected for albedo.
  - The MD3 and MDR colour maps (low-incidence campaigns, averaged over many images) are the candidates. Their residual shading is to measure against slopes from the DEM before W3 chooses.
- **Mercury's brightness:** Mallama & Hilton (2018) Eq. 2 (phase 2.1–169.5°) and Mallama et al. (2017) Table 7 (V 0.142) were read.
  - Domingue et al. (2016), whose law the maps were corrected with, has no open copy. The law will be fitted to Eq. 2 as Mars's was.
- **Checksums:** the PDS copies publish MD5s for every file; the USGS GeoTIFFs do not. Downloads use the PDS copies, or pin the USGS ones at first retrieval.

## W2 · Constants and ephemeris

Owner, 2026-10-07: "Next story".

`pipeline/ephemeris_mercury.py` writes `public/data/mercury/ephemeris.json`. Run it with `npm run pipeline:ephemeris:mercury`; CI re-derives the output and diffs it.

**Kernels:** the Moon's, already pinned: `naif0012.tls`, `de440s.bsp` (it holds Mercury, 199), `pck00011.tpc` and `gm_de440.tpc`. Nothing new to download.

**Written:**
- **Shape:** radii 2440.53 × 2440.53 × 2438.26 km (`BODY199_RADII`).
- **Gravity:** GM 22,031.87 km³/s² (`BODY199_GM`).
- **Body frame:** `IAU_MERCURY`, planetocentric, east-positive.
- **Rotation model:** the pole and prime meridian, and the libration in longitude: five periodic terms on `BODY1_NUT_PREC_ANGLES`, the largest 0.0107°.
  - The app evaluates them with the code that turns Mars (`core/mars.ts` `marsRotation`).
  - `src/core/mercury.test.ts` shows it reproduces SPICE's matrices at both epochs within 1e-9.
- **At each canonical epoch:**
  - The Sun's and Earth's directions and distances.
  - The rotation into `IAU_MERCURY`.
  - Mercury's distance from the Sun and its true anomaly. Mercury has almost no axial tilt, so its "seasons" are its distance from the Sun, 0.31 to 0.47 AU.

| Epoch | UTC | Sun distance | True anomaly | Sub-solar point seen from Earth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `full-2026-01` | 2026-01-03 10:00 | 0.465 AU | 171.6° (near aphelion) | 100.40° E, 0.02° S |
| `first-quarter-2026-01` | 2026-01-26 05:00 | 0.415 AU | 239.0° | 27.91° E, 0.03° S |

### Checked against JPL Horizons

Fixture: `test/fixtures/horizons-mercury.json`, with the requests and Horizons' own header conventions recorded. Test: `pipeline/test_ephemeris_mercury.py`.

| Check | Measured agreement | Tolerance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sub-solar point seen from Earth | 1.3e-6° and 1.6e-6° | 0.1° (the Moon's and Mars's) |
| The shipped vectors (Sun direction × rotation matrix), against the sub-solar point seen from the Sun | 0.009° and 0.006° | 0.1° |

- **Conventions:**
  - Horizons gives Mercury's longitudes west-positive ("Target pole/equ : IAU_MERCURY {West-longitude positive}") and its latitudes planetodetic. The test converts both.
  - A control test shows the check fails without the conversion.
- **Light time:** Earth sees Mercury 5–10 minutes late. Mercury turns only 6.1° a day, so unlike Mars (4.87° off at 20 minutes) that is about 0.02°.
  - The app's vectors are still checked from the nearer observer, the Sun (3–4 minutes). The remaining 0.006–0.009° is that light time.

**Not checked here:** whether the MDIS maps' longitudes match `IAU_MERCURY`. They were made with MESSENGER-era kernels. W3 checks them against the Gazetteer's features on rendered images, as Mars's maps were.

## W3 · True albedo: the candidates measured

Owner, 2026-10-08: "Next story".

**The question:** which of MESSENGER's maps is Mercury's true brightness, with no lighting baked in? Every MDIS map is "reflectance corrected to i = 30º, e = 0º, g = 30º", but the correction does not remove what the slopes did to the light when the images were taken. So the residual shading had to be measured.

**First attempt, against slopes from the DEM: failed, and the reason is recorded.**
- **The method:** each map's local brightness, at 16 px/° (2.7 km), was regressed on the east-facing and equator-facing slopes of the USGS DEM.
- **The result:** every map, the BDR included, showed under 1% brightness per degree of slope, with R² ≤ 0.025.
- **Why it failed:** the BDR plainly has deep shadows (`ss16-albedo-candidates.png`). At 665 m the DEM does not resolve the craters that cast them. The method cannot see shading, so its numbers are not evidence.

**Second method, against the shading MDIS itself mapped.**
- **The template:** MDIS mapped the same 750 nm reflectance twice at incidence 78–86°, once lit from the east (HIE) and once from the west (HIW). Their normalised difference, (HIW − HIE)/(HIW + HIE), cancels albedo and keeps shading.
- **Read from PDS:** 2,048 rows of tile `H10NW2` of each, by HTTP range request, at 20–28° E, 2–10° N, 256 px/°. The label gives little-endian float32, band-sequential, equirectangular centred on 18° E, line projection offset 5760.5.
- **What the region showed:** HIW is filled with low-incidence images there (`ss16-albedo-shading.png`). So the template is HIE's shading, with HIW's albedo partly in it. Each map's local brightness, regressed on the template:

| Map | Coefficient | Correlation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HIE (east-lit, the reference) | −1.66 | −0.96 |
| BDR (i ≈ 74°) | −1.25 | −0.80 |
| LOI (i ≈ 45°) | +0.12 | +0.23 |
| MD3 (lowest incidence, 665 m) | +0.21 | +0.29 |

**What the numbers say:**
- **BDR carries about three quarters of a deliberately low-Sun map's shading.** It is lit from the same side as HIE in this region. Rejected, as W1 expected.
- **LOI and MD3 carry at most about a tenth of it.** Their small positive coefficients have the opposite sign to shading: albedo shared with HIW, not light. The images show their craters without shadows.
- **LOI has visible seams** where its 166 m tiles meet: straight edges in brightness that are not terrain (`ss16-albedo-candidates.png`, `ss16-albedo-shading.png`).
- **MD3 is softer (665 m in the USGS copy, 330 m in PDS) and in colour:** 430, 750 and 1000 nm, averaged over many images, so no seams show.

**The website's albedo is about 1 km a pixel anyway:** the app's 8192 × 4096 texture over Mercury. So for the website the choice is about seams, colour and residual shading, not detail. Local mode (W5) can use the finer maps.

**Coverage, found when building:** MD3 has 45 tiles, from the north pole (`H01NP2`, polar stereographic, to 53.8° N) down to 43.755° S (`H11`–`H14` NE and NW only). It has nothing further south.
- **MDR v4** (`MSGRMDS_5001`, 64 px/°, 17 bands) is global: 58 tiles, including the south polar tile `H15SP4` and its gap-filling `_2700_` version.
- **MDR has the same three filters among its eight:** 430 nm (WAC 6), 750 nm (WAC 7), 1000 nm (WAC 9). Same normalisation (i = 30°, e = 0°, g = 30°), same Kaasalainen–Shkuratov model.

## W3 · True albedo: built

`pipeline/mercury.py` (`npm run pipeline:mercury`) writes `public/data/mercury/albedo.webp`, `albedo-colour.png`, `albedo-source.png` and `albedo.json`. It is too large for CI (about 27 GB of tiles), so `pipeline/test_mercury.py` checks the committed files against the manifest.

**Sources:** `pipeline/mercury_sources.json` lists all 100 tiles, 45 MD3 and 55 MDR. Each streams from PDS Imaging once and is checked against its published MD5. Only its three colour bands are kept, averaged to 32 px/°.

**Conventions,** from every tile's PDS3 label:
- Little-endian float32 (`PC_REAL`), band-sequential, units "Reflectance".
- Equirectangular or polar stereographic, on a 2439.4 km sphere, planetocentric, east-positive.
- The output grid: column 0 = −180°, row 0 = +90°.

**Tile geometry, checked:**
- **Equirectangular tiles:** by the label formula, every tile's edges fall within 0.0 px of their labelled latitude limits.
- **Polar stereographic tiles:** each of the eight ways to lay the projection on the tile was tried against the equatorial tiles it overlaps. The polar tiles' data stop near 64°; their labelled 53.8° is only the square's corner. The standard PDS orientation won every time, and no other scored above 0.13:

| Tile | Correlation |
| --- | --- |
| MD3 north | 0.946 |
| MDR north | 0.941 |
| MDR south | 0.888 |
| MDR south, 2700 m version | 0.940 |

**Coverage was not what the labels suggested.** MD3's tiles reach 43.8° S, but its data thin out well before that:

| Latitude | MD3 coverage |
| --- | --- |
| 0–10° S | 94% |
| 20–30° S | 65% |
| 30–40° S | 23% |
| South of 40° S | nothing |

- **By the owner's rule** ("where only one product measured, that one"), calibrated MDR fills every place MD3 did not measure.
- **No seam shows** where the two meet.
- **Result by area:**
  - MD3: 72.3%.
  - MDR, calibrated: 27.4%.
  - Blend across 38–43° S: 0.1% (MD3 barely reaches it).
  - Gap: 0.2%, at the poles, where neither product measured. Shown as missing.

**Cross-calibration,** MD3 = gain × MDR per band, least squares through the origin, over 20–37° S (1.58 million pixels):

| Band | Gain | Median ratio | Correlation | RMS residual | Gain by 5° strip |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 430 nm | 1.0054 | 1.0041 | 0.958 | 5.8% | 1.004–1.008 |
| 750 nm | 1.0078 | 1.0075 | 0.955 | 4.6% | 1.005–1.011 |
| 1000 nm | 1.0079 | 1.0078 | 0.952 | 4.2% | 1.005–1.009 |

The two products already agree to under 1%. The gain is stable from strip to strip.

**MD3 against MDR,** two independently assembled PDS products, 750 nm, 4 px/°:
- **Correlation:** 0.98–0.99 from 60° N to 45° S, 0.958 at 60–75° N, 0.853 above 75° N.

**Against USGS's own mosaic of MD3** (the 8-bit GeoTIFF):
- **The check:** 0.970 over 45° N to 40° S. The map mirrored east–west scores 0.012, and shifted 1° east 0.687, so a mirrored or misplaced map would fail.
- **The north:** USGS's copy agrees less there: 0.90 at 45–60° N, 0.78 at 60–75° N, 0.38 above. Two reasons:
  - Its 8-bit stretch clips much of the far north: at 85° N a third of the pixels read 1 and a sixth 255. Cells holding a clipped value are not compared.
  - Its north shows strong crater shading that neither PDS product has (MD3 against MDR is 0.85–0.96 there).
- **So the 0.9 test is applied south of 45° N, and every band is recorded.** I chose that limit after seeing the failure. It needs the owner's approval in the PR, as any check does.

**Encoding:**
- **Albedo:** reflectance at 750 nm, byte = 255 √(R / 0.37), 0 = not measured. 0.37 is the 99.99th percentile. Median 0.0704.
- **File size:** lossless 12.4 MB (owner).
- **Colour:** 2048 × 1024. Red = R(1000)/R(750) over 1.082–1.355; green = R(430)/R(750) over 0.394–0.687.

**Known limits:**
- **The colour ratios show image footprints when stretched:** straight-edged patches from per-image calibration within MD3 itself, a few per cent of tint. They are kept as measured, not smoothed.
- **Near both poles crater rims stay bright:** the only images there were taken with the Sun low. W1 expected this.

**Not checked here:**
- Whether the map's longitudes match `IAU_MERCURY` against the Gazetteer's features. That is for W7, on rendered images.
- How the textures look in the app. Nothing renders them yet; W7 builds the page.
